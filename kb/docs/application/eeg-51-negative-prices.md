# EEG section 51 and negative prices

The 2025 amendment of the Renewable Energy Sources Act (the "Solarspitzengesetz") changed what
German renewable plants earn when prices are negative.

- Plants commissioned after 25 February 2025 receive no market premium and no feed-in tariff in
  any quarter hour with a negative day-ahead price, whatever its length.
- The quarter hours lost this way are added to the end of the 20-year support period
  (section 51a), so support is deferred rather than cut, for plants with a smart meter.
- Older plants keep the rules they were commissioned under, where support stopped only after
  longer runs of negative prices.
- New small PV systems without a smart meter and control box may feed in at most 60 percent of
  their peak capacity.

Why it matters for prices: plants without a premium to lose have no reason to keep producing
below zero, so they curtail at zero. The larger the share of such plants, the more surplus is
absorbed at zero and the shallower negative prices become; prices go deeply negative only when
the surplus exceeds what curtails at zero.

Why it matters for PPAs: a pay-as-produced PPA with a negative-price clause neither delivers nor
pays in negative quarter hours, mirroring the support rule. The clause removes the worst-priced
volume, so the PPA's capture price is higher than the zone's technology capture price.
